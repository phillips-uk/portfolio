When you ask me a question

The "Ask me a question" form collects your name, your email address, your business or website if you add it, your question, the page you sent it from and the country your connection comes from. I use these only to reply to you and to keep track of the conversation, including any work that follows. The lawful basis is my legitimate interest in answering questions people send me, and taking steps you ask for before any contract.

Your question is stored on Cloudflare, emailed to me through Resend, and my reply comes from Google Workspace email. I keep it for as long as it is useful for the conversation and any work that follows, then delete it.

When you sign up to The Monday Brief

The sign-up form collects your email address. I also record when and on which page you signed up, the wording you agreed to, and when you confirmed. Nobody is added to the list until they click the link in the confirmation email. The lawful basis is your consent.

The list and the emails are handled by Resend; the sign-up records are stored on Cloudflare. Every email has an unsubscribe link, and unsubscribing takes effect straight away. Sign-ups that are never confirmed are deleted after 7 days. If you unsubscribe, I keep your address only on a do-not-email list, so you aren't emailed again. You can sign up again at any time.

When you use the free tools

Website Check. You enter a web address. The check reads that public page and a few public files from the same site (robots.txt, the sitemap and llms.txt), and sends the address to Google's PageSpeed Insights to measure its speed. The report is stored on Cloudflare for 30 days so its link works, then deleted.

Google Ads Check. You enter your Google Ads customer ID and invite my manager account from inside Google Ads. The check reads the account through the Google Ads API (campaigns, settings, spend and results, conversions, search terms, keywords, ads and products), then removes the link. It changes nothing in your account. The report is stored on Cloudflare for 30 days so its link works, then deleted. To allow one free check per account every 30 days, I keep a scrambled, one-way form of the account ID for up to a year.

The lawful basis for both is my legitimate interest in providing the check you asked for. If you then send me a question about a report, the section above applies.

Sign in with Google

The Google Ads Check can also start with "Sign in with Google" instead of inviting my manager account. Google then asks you to allow the app "Phillips." to use your Google Ads data. Google offers only one permission for Google Ads, which allows both reading and making changes; there is no read-only version.

What is read, and why. The list of Google Ads accounts your login can open (their names and IDs), so you can choose one, and then the account you choose: the same data as the manager-link check above, to write your report.

How the permission is held. Google gives the check a short-lived access token. It is kept only in an encrypted cookie in your own browser, for up to 15 minutes, and deleted as soon as the check runs or you sign out. It is never stored on my side, and no long-lived token is requested, so the check cannot reach your account later.

Changes. The check only reads. Nothing in your account is changed unless you buy a fix and approve the exact list of changes on screen first.

Who it is shared with. Cloudflare hosts this site and stores the report, acting only on my instructions. No one else receives your Google Ads data. I never sell it, and never pass it to advertisers, data brokers or any AI service.

How it is protected. Everything travels over HTTPS. The access token is encrypted (AES-GCM) inside a cookie that the page's scripts cannot read, and your browser sends it only over HTTPS and only to the check's own addresses. Reports are kept in private Cloudflare storage that only I can reach, and each one opens only through its own randomly generated link.

Reports and removing access. The report is kept for 30 days so its link works, then deleted, as above. You can remove the app's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Phillips.'s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Information from Google is used only to provide the check you asked for: it is never sold, never used for advertising and never used to train AI models.

Stopping spam

Both forms limit how many times they can be used from one connection in an hour, and the Website Check limits how many checks one connection can run in a day. To do that they store a scrambled, one-way form of your connection's address. The address itself is not stored.

The "Ask me a question" form and the free tools also use Cloudflare Turnstile to check for automated traffic. Cloudflare says it processes only the information it needs to tell people from automated traffic and does not read what you type into the form. Most people never see it; now and then it may ask you to tick a box.

Analytics

This site uses Google Analytics, through Google Tag Manager, to see which pages people visit and which forms they use. Google Analytics uses cookies and collects details such as the pages you view, your device and browser, and your approximate location. Google provides the service. You can stop it in your browser settings or with Google's opt-out add-on.

Hosting

Cloudflare hosts this website. It processes technical details of each visit, such as your IP address, to deliver the pages and protect the site.

Who else sees your data

Only the providers named above, each acting for me: Cloudflare (hosting, storage and spam checks), Resend (email) and Google (email, analytics, and the Google Ads and PageSpeed Insights services the free tools use). Some of them are based outside the UK, including in the United States. I don't sell your data or share it with anyone else for their own marketing.

Your rights

You can ask for a copy of the data I hold about you, ask me to correct or delete it, object to how I use it, or withdraw your consent at any time. Email the address under "Who I am" or ask me a question. If you're unhappy with how I've handled your data, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.
