What this website collects about you, why, who it goes to and how long it is kept. Last updated 22 November 2026.

When you ask me a question

The "Ask me a question" form collects your name, your email address, your business or website if you add it, your question, the page you sent it from and the country your connection comes from. I use these only to reply to you and to keep track of the conversation, including any work that follows. The lawful basis is my legitimate interest in answering questions people send me, and taking steps you ask for before any contract.

Your question is stored on Cloudflare, emailed to me through Resend, and my reply comes from Google Workspace email. I keep it for as long as it is useful for the conversation and any work that follows, then delete it.

When you sign up to The Monday Brief

The sign-up form collects your email address. I also record when and on which page you signed up, the wording you agreed to, and when you confirmed. Nobody is added to the list until they click the link in the confirmation email. The lawful basis is your consent.

The list and the emails are handled by Resend; the sign-up records are stored on Cloudflare. Every email has an unsubscribe link, and unsubscribing takes effect straight away. Sign-ups that are never confirmed are deleted after 7 days. If you unsubscribe, I keep your address only on a do-not-email list, so you aren't emailed again. You can sign up again at any time.

When you use the free tools

Website Check. You enter a web address. The check reads up to 25 public pages of that site, tests whether up to 25 of its other links still work, and reads a few public files (robots.txt, the sitemap and llms.txt). It also sends the address to Google's PageSpeed Insights to measure speed. The report is stored on Cloudflare for 30 days so its link works, then deleted.

So that the next check of the same site can show what has changed, I keep a short record of its latest scores and which checks it passed, with no page content. It is deleted about a year after the site was last checked.

Google Ads Check. You enter your Google Ads customer ID and invite my manager account from inside Google Ads. The check reads the account through the Google Ads API (campaigns, settings, spend and results, conversions, search terms, keywords, ads and products), then removes the link. It changes nothing in your account. The report is stored on Cloudflare for 30 days so its link works, then deleted. To allow one free check per account every 30 days, I keep a scrambled, one-way form of the account ID for up to a year.

The lawful basis for both is my legitimate interest in providing the check you asked for. If you then send me a question about a report, the section on questions above applies.

Sign in with Google

The Google Ads Check can also start with "Sign in with Google" instead of inviting my manager account. Google then asks you to allow the app "Phillips." to use your Google Ads data. Google offers only one permission for Google Ads, which allows both reading and making changes; there is no read-only version.

What is read, and why. The list of Google Ads accounts your login can open (their names and IDs), so you can choose one, and then the account you choose: the same data as the manager-link check above, to write your report.

How the permission is held. Google gives the check a short-lived access token. It is kept only in an encrypted cookie in your own browser, for up to 15 minutes, and deleted as soon as the check runs or you sign out. It is never stored on my side, and no long-lived token is requested, so the check cannot reach your account later.

Changes. The check only reads. Nothing in your account is changed unless you buy a fix and approve the exact list of changes on screen first.

Who it is shared with. Cloudflare hosts this site and stores the report, acting only on my instructions. No one else receives your Google Ads data. I never sell it, and never pass it to advertisers, data brokers or any AI service.

How it is protected. Everything travels over HTTPS. The access token is encrypted (AES-GCM) inside a cookie that the page's scripts cannot read, and your browser sends it only over HTTPS and only to the check's own addresses. Reports are kept in private Cloudflare storage that only I can reach, and each one opens only through its own randomly generated link.

Reports and removing access. The report is kept for 30 days so its link works, then deleted, as above. You can remove the app's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Phillips.'s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Information from Google is used only to provide the check you asked for: it is never sold, never used for advertising and never used to train AI models.

Accounts

Accounts are optional and in a private preview, so they are not open to visitors yet. Every free check works without one. This section says how they will work.

What is kept. Your email address; your name and Google account ID if you sign in with Google; when the account was made and last used; and the checks you keep with it (the site or Google Ads account checked, the date, the scores and the report). Saved checks are not deleted after 30 days: they are kept for as long as the account exists.

Signing in. You can sign in with a link sent to your email address, or with Google, which shares your name, email address and Google account ID with me. Each email link works once, for 20 minutes. Only a scrambled, one-way form of it is stored, and that is deleted within about a week. A signed cookie keeps you signed in for 30 days.

Where it is kept. Accounts are stored in a Cloudflare database in Western Europe, and saved reports in Cloudflare storage. Sign-in emails are sent by Cloudflare's email service from my contact address.

Why. The lawful basis is providing the account you asked for. A button to delete your account and everything kept with it, and one to download a copy of your data, are coming before accounts open. Until then, ask me and I will do either for you.

Cookies

Essential cookies. Some cookies are needed for the site to work, so I don't ask for them. A cookie called ph_consent remembers your cookie choice for six months; it holds only the choice and the version of the wording you saw. ph_acct keeps you signed in to your account for 30 days. ph_gads and ph_gads_oauth hold the Sign in with Google step of the Google Ads Check, for 15 minutes at most. Cloudflare Turnstile may store information in your browser on the forms and free tools to stop spam. I keep no record of your cookie choice beyond the ph_consent cookie on your own device.

Site usage statistics. I use Google Analytics, through Google Tag Manager, to count visits and see which pages, links and forms people use (never what you type), so I can improve the site. It sets cookies whose names start with _ga, which last up to two years. Google receives the address of the page, your browser and device type, and an approximate location worked out from your IP address. Google handles them for me as a processor, and I use these statistics only to improve the site, not for advertising. The lawful basis is my legitimate interest in understanding how the site is used. Statistics are on unless you turn them off: you can do that from the banner on your first visit, or at any time in Cookie settings, also at the bottom of every page. Turning them off stops Google Analytics on this site and removes its cookies; the site works the same without them. Google’s opt-out add-on also works.

Advertising. This site runs no advertising cookies or tags. If that changes, they will be off unless you say yes, and this notice will say so first.

Private pages shared by link

Some pages, such as a proposal, open only through a private link I send. They set one cookie, which lets you reopen the page for up to a year. Each time the page is opened, I record the time, the kind of device, its browser and operating system, the approximate city, region and country supplied by Cloudflare, and the website that linked to it if there was one. I don't store your IP address. While the page is open, it tells me how many seconds it was on screen and how far down it was scrolled, so I can see whether a proposal was read. I send Google Analytics the page's address without the private link, subject to your cookie choice.

Stopping spam

Both forms limit how many times they can be used from one connection in an hour, and the Website Check limits how many checks one connection can run in a day. To do that they store a scrambled, one-way form of your connection's address. The address itself is not stored.

The "Ask me a question" form and the free tools also use Cloudflare Turnstile to check for automated traffic. Cloudflare says it processes only the information it needs to tell people from automated traffic and does not read what you type into the form. Most people never see it; now and then it may ask you to tick a box.

Hosting

Cloudflare hosts this website. It processes technical details of each visit, such as your IP address, to deliver the pages and protect the site.

Who else sees your data

Only the providers named above, each acting for me: Cloudflare (hosting, storage, the accounts database, sign-in emails and spam checks), Resend (email) and Google (email, analytics, Sign in with Google, and the Google Ads and PageSpeed Insights services the free tools use). Some of them process information outside the UK, including in the United States. Where they do, the transfer relies on the arrangements in each provider's data processing terms: UK adequacy regulations, or contractual safeguards such as the UK Addendum to the standard contractual clauses. You can ask me for details of the safeguards and how to get a copy. I don't sell your data or share it with anyone else for their own marketing.
