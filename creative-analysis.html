THE SCORING RUBRIC

Five dimensions. One composite score.

Hook Quality
Does it stop the scroll? Pattern interrupt, emotional trigger, or strong curiosity signal in the first frame or above-fold.
Visual Hierarchy
Is there a clear focal point? Is the reading order obvious without effort?
Copy Clarity
Is the core message understood in under two seconds? No decoding required.
Brand Consistency
Coherent colours, fonts, and tone. Does it read as a brand or as noise?
Fatigue Signals
Absence of overused formats: talking-head UGC with no hook, plain white product shots, generic CTAs, logo-first layouts, low contrast, text-heavy compositions. A high score means low fatigue risk.

Weak signal flagged for any dimension scoring 6 or below. Composite = equal-weighted average across all five. Score from 1 to 10.

WHAT TO KNOW

A few things worth knowing before you run it.

VIDEO SUPPORT Video files are supported. Three frames are extracted per video — opening, midpoint, and closing — and each is scored independently. The composite reflects all three.
JUDGMENT CONTEXT Scores are AI-generated using a consistent rubric. They are reproducible and documented, which is the point — but they should be used alongside campaign performance data, not as a replacement for it.
FORMAT AGNOSTIC The rubric applies the same logic to Meta, Google, and TikTok creatives. Platform-specific attention patterns are not currently scored separately — that is a future scope item.
WHAT IT IS NOT This is not a real-time audience measurement tool. It scores creative signals, not ad performance. A creative can score well here and still underperform if targeting or audience quality is the problem.
AD CREATIVE ANALYSIS

Score your paid ad creatives before they go live

Most creative review processes are subjective. A senior marketer looks at a set of ads and gives a verdict based on experience and instinct. That works — until you are running 30 variants across four campaigns and need a consistent framework for prioritising which creatives to pause, which to iterate on, and which to scale.

This tool applies a consistent five-dimension rubric to every creative you upload. Each image or video frame is scored on hook quality, visual hierarchy, copy clarity, brand consistency, and fatigue signals. The composite score gives you a ranked view across a batch. The weak signal flags tell you exactly what to fix.

What makes a paid creative fail? The most common failure modes are not about production quality. They are about stopping the scroll, communicating the offer in under two seconds, and avoiding the formats that audiences have learned to ignore. This tool is built around those three problems.

What is ad creative fatigue?

Creative fatigue describes the point at which an audience has seen a format, style, or message often enough that engagement drops — not because the product is less appealing, but because the creative has lost its ability to interrupt. Common signals include generic UGC formats with no hook, logo-first compositions, plain white product shots without context, and overused CTA phrases like "Shop now" or "Learn more".

How does AI scoring differ from a manual creative review?

A manual review depends on the reviewer's frame of reference and changes between sessions. An AI-scored rubric applies the same criteria every time, returns a documented rationale for each score, and flags specific weak signals rather than general impressions. The output is the same whether you score 5 creatives or 50.

Can I use this to brief new creatives?

The weak signal flags are designed to feed directly into briefs. If Hook Quality scores 4 with a note about "no pattern interrupt in the first frame", that is a specific instruction for the next creative — not a general note about "making it more engaging".

What do I do with a low-scoring creative?

Check which dimension is pulling the composite score down. A low Hook Quality score points to the opening frame. A low Copy Clarity score points to the headline or body copy. A low Fatigue Signals score means the format itself may need to change, not just the copy or image.
