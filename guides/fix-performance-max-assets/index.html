How to Fix Performance Max Asset Issues in Google Ads (2026)

Performance Max builds its ads from what you give it. Leave a gap and it fills it itself, with its own videos, its own copy and its own choice of landing page. This guide puts Google's asset requirements in one place, explains what Ad Strength really tells you, and fixes the nine asset issues that come up most.

Check my Google Ads

Lewis Phillips · 17 Nov 2026 · Updated 21 Nov 2026 · 10 min read

Quick answer

Fill every asset group to Google's recommended counts: 11 or more headlines, 2 long headlines, 4 descriptions, 4 landscape and 4 square images, 2 portrait images, a logo, and your own video in horizontal, square and vertical. Then check the settings Google switches on for you: auto-generated video, Final URL expansion, text customisation and image enhancements. Treat Ad Strength as a coverage check, not a score to chase.

What an asset group is, and why it matters

An asset group is the set of headlines, descriptions, images, logos and videos Performance Max builds its ads from, around one theme or one set of products. Google mixes and matches them into ads for Search, YouTube, Display, Discover, Gmail and Maps.

That makes the assets the brief. A Search campaign mixes the headlines and descriptions you wrote. Performance Max chooses the combination it thinks will work. Depending on your settings, Google can generate eligible videos or copy and choose a different landing page. Most asset issues come down to one of those gaps, or to a setting Google switched on for you.

What Ad Strength tells you, and what it doesn't

Every asset group gets an Ad Strength rating. Google scores it on the asset types you have, how varied they are, how different your text is, and how many assets there are in total.

  • Incomplete: missing required creative or a call or lead form asset required by the primary bidding goal. The asset group can’t run.
  • Poor: the minimum only, so it can serve on some placements but not all.
  • Average: every asset type is there, with little variety.
  • Good: every type, with good variety and different text.
  • Excellent: every type including the optional ones, 6 or more sitelinks, your own videos in all three shapes, and all 15 headline and 5 description slots used.

Google calls it an indicator. It has found that higher Ad Strength goes with better performance, but the rating itself isn't a bid or an auction score. I use it as a coverage check: Poor or Average almost always means a missing asset type, or headlines that repeat each other. Two details catch people out. Text that Google writes for you counts towards the rating. Videos Google makes for you don't count towards Excellent.

Nine common asset issues, and how to fix each

Each one below has what you see in the account, why it happens and the fix. Settings live under each campaign's Settings, in the Asset optimisation section, unless I say otherwise.

01 Ad Strength stuck at Poor or Average

A simple test for variety: read the headlines out loud. If three of them are the brand name with a different adjective, merge them and use the space for a price, a guarantee, a location or a review.

02 Google's videos running instead of yours

There is no separate switch for this. Google's own guidance is that uploading your own videos in all three shapes is what stops auto-generated ones. If you have no video at all, the video creation tool inside Google Ads can build a simple one from your own images, which at least keeps the content yours.

Video enhancements are a different setting: these are Google's reworked versions and shorter cuts of videos you uploaded. You can untick them under Asset optimisation, then Video.

03 Ads sending people to the wrong pages

Google's own advice for a business with one landing page is to keep text customisation on and turn Final URL expansion off. Where several pages are fair game, keep expansion on and add individual URLs or rules under Asset optimisation, such as excluding anything under /blog/. You can also exclude pages by label if you use a page feed in place of a Merchant Center, vehicle or hotel feed.

Three things to know. Exclusions only work while expansion is on. You can't exclude the asset group's own final URL. Changes can take 24 to 48 hours to apply. To see where traffic actually went, open the Expanded Final URL assets view for the campaign.

04 Copy you didn't write

Removing a single Google-written asset is permanent for that asset. To switch the whole feature off, untick it under Asset optimisation, Text customisation. Text guidelines, where your account has them, are a gentler option: you can exclude up to 25 terms per campaign (a competitor's name, a word your industry can't use) and add plain-English messaging restrictions such as "don't mention discounts". I leave text customisation on where the site is accurate and current, and switch it off for regulated businesses where one wrong phrase is a compliance problem.

05 Images limited or disapproved

Text and logos laid over an image are allowed in Performance Max, with limits. Google recommends at least one clean image without overlays for each shape, and keeping the important part of the picture in the middle 80%, because placements crop the edges. A clear, single photo of the product or the work usually beats a designed banner.

06 The wrong business name or logo

The name must match your domain or your verified legal name, and the logo must appear on your landing page. Allow up to two working days for Google to review uploaded business information assets. If old logos keep appearing, check the account-level Dynamic business logos setting. Colours and fonts only apply to Google's own videos and to display ads, so they matter most when issue 02 isn't fixed yet.

07 Images Google changed or added

08 One asset group doing every job

A jeweller selling rings and cufflinks, or a firm offering two different services, is selling to different people with different words. Separate asset groups let each set of assets, images and landing pages match. Each can take up to 50 search themes, which tell Google what the group is about. Don't split so far that each group has too little to learn from: themes should differ in message, not just in name.

09 No idea which assets work

Read the conversion numbers carefully. Google doesn't split a conversion between the assets that made the ad, so the asset totals add up to more than the asset group's. Plenty of assets show no conversions simply because they rarely served. I compare assets of the same type over a few weeks, and replace the ones that get impressions and nothing else.

How I check an asset group

This is the order I go through when I open a Performance Max campaign. It takes each issue above in turn.

  1. Status and Ad Strength for every asset group. Anything Incomplete or Poor goes to the top of the list.
  2. Count each asset type against the table above, including portrait images, long headlines and sitelinks.
  3. Videos: are they yours, in all three shapes, or Google's?
  4. Asset optimisation settings: Final URL expansion and exclusions, text customisation, video enhancements and image options.
  5. Where people landed: the expanded URLs, checked against the pages you want to sell from.
  6. Policy: anything Limited or Disapproved, fixed or appealed.
  7. The asset report: what served, what converted, and what to replace next.

Questions

Does Ad Strength affect how Performance Max performs?

Not directly. Google calls Ad Strength an indicator of how complete and varied your assets are, not an auction factor. It has found that higher Ad Strength goes with better performance, so a low rating is worth fixing, but by adding real, varied assets rather than writing to the score. An Incomplete asset group can’t run. Check for missing required creative and for any call or lead form asset required by the primary bidding goal.

How do I stop Performance Max making its own videos?

Upload your own. Google makes videos when an asset group has no active video of its own, and its guidance is that uploading your own in all three shapes (horizontal, square and vertical, each at least 10 seconds long) stops that. Keep the vertical video between 10 and 60 seconds for Shorts eligibility. There is no separate off switch for auto-generated video.

Should I turn off Final URL expansion?

Turn it off if one page is the only route to a sale or enquiry: that is Google's own advice, with text customisation left on. If several pages on your site are good landing pages, keep it on and exclude the ones you don't want, such as blog posts, job pages and old offers. Final URL expansion is unavailable in campaigns that use store goals only.

How many headlines does Performance Max need?

The standard requirement is 3 to 15 headlines, each up to 30 characters. Google recommends 11 or more, with at least one of 15 characters or fewer. The standard requirements also include 1 to 5 long headlines and 2 to 5 descriptions, each up to 90 characters. Campaigns linked to a Merchant Center feed can launch without additional creative assets.

What image sizes does Performance Max need?

For a standard asset group, landscape 1.91:1 (1200 x 628 recommended) and square 1:1 (1200 x 1200 recommended) are required. Portrait 4:5 (960 x 1200 recommended) is optional. You can add up to 20 images of each format per asset group, each up to 5,120 KB. The standard logo requirement is a square 1:1 version, with an optional landscape 4:1 version. Campaigns linked to a Merchant Center feed can launch without additional creative assets.
