Google Ads Audit Checklist: 9 Areas to Check, in Order

Lewis Phillips · 17 Nov 2026 · Updated 21 Nov 2026 · 11 min read

This is the checklist I work through when I take on a Google Ads account. Nine areas, in the order I check them, each with what a pass and a fail look like. Work through it yourself, or use it to check the work of whoever runs your account.

Quick answer

Audit in this order: conversion tracking, bidding and budget, account structure, keywords and search terms, ads and assets, targeting and audiences, Performance Max, product feed and landing pages. Fix anything that corrupts the data first, then anything that wastes money now, then anything holding back growth. Do a full audit once or twice a year and a short check of tracking, budgets and search terms every month.

Why audit, and how often

Google Ads accounts rarely break in one go. A tracking tag stops firing after a website update, a budget cap that made sense last year stays in place, search terms drift, and nobody notices because the account still spends and still reports something.

An audit is a structured look at the whole account against a fixed list, so problems are found by checking rather than by luck. I do a full one when I take on an account, after big changes such as a new website or new tracking, and once or twice a year after that. In between, a short monthly check of tracking, budgets and search terms catches most problems early.

The nine areas below are in the order I work through them. The order matters: there is no point tuning bids or ads while the account is learning from the wrong conversions.

01

Conversion tracking

Everything else depends on this. Smart Bidding learns from the conversions it sees, so if they are wrong, the bidding chases the wrong thing and every report misleads.

A pass
A fail
What counts as a conversion
The actions used for bidding record real enquiries, qualifying calls or sales.
Page views, button clicks or time on site used for bidding as if they were enquiries or sales.
Action optimisation (Primary/Secondary)
In Goals → Conversions → Summary, check each action’s Action optimisation setting and which goals each campaign uses. Smart Bidding uses Primary actions in selected standard goals and all actions in selected custom goals, including Secondary actions.
Overlapping actions used for bidding, so one enquiry counts twice.
Google Analytics imports
If you import GA4 conversions, check Action optimisation in Goals → Conversions → Summary. Actions created in Google Analytics start as Secondary. Choose deliberately which to make Primary and which to include in any custom goal.
GA4 and Google Ads tags both counting the same purchase for bidding.
Attribution
Main conversions use data-driven attribution, Google's default for most actions.
Old last-click settings left on actions that drive bidding.
Enhanced conversions
For lead businesses, the site collects eligible customer details and closed sales are uploaded as offline conversions, so Google Ads can credit them to the ads.
No way to tell which enquiries became customers.
Worth knowing

Google Ads can use hashed first-party data, such as an email address from a lead form, to match offline sales back to the campaign that brought the lead. For any business where the sale happens after the enquiry, this is the difference between bidding for form fills and bidding for customers.

02

Bidding and budget

Once tracking is right, check that the bid strategy suits the volume of conversions and that budgets go where they earn.

A pass
A fail
Strategy fits volume
A conversion-based strategy with enough conversions to learn from; a target set from what the account really achieves.
Target CPA or ROAS on a campaign with a handful of conversions a month and a target pulled from a wish list.
Spend that earns
Every campaign that spends brings in conversions, or has a clear reason not to yet.
Campaigns spending month after month with nothing to show.
Budget limits
Your best campaigns are not Limited by budget. Google warns a limited budget can mean fewer clicks and impressions than you could have earned.
The most profitable campaign capped while weaker ones spend freely.
Manual adjustments
Smart Bidding generally handles bid adjustments automatically. With Target CPA, a device adjustment changes the CPA target for that device. A supported −100% adjustment can exclude a device.
Relying on old adjustments the bid strategy ignores, or changing a device’s CPA target by mistake.

For choosing a strategy, see which bidding strategy to use and when. For judging one on thin data, Target CPA with few conversions.

Account structure

Structure decides how much data each campaign has to learn from. Google's own advice has moved towards fewer, broader campaigns organised around business goals.

Check
A pass
A fail
Campaigns by goal
Campaigns split where budgets or targets genuinely differ: a service, a region, a margin.
Dozens of small campaigns split by habit, each too thin for Smart Bidding.
Themed ad groups
Ad groups built around one theme that shares an ad and a landing page.
Single-keyword ad groups, or one ad group holding everything.
Match type overlap
Repeated keywords have a clear purpose. Broader match types already cover the searches eligible under narrower match types.
The same keyword repeated in exact, phrase and broad across ad groups without a clear reason.
Nothing empty
Every enabled ad group has eligible ads and the targeting it needs, such as keywords or dynamic targets.

Keywords and search terms

This is where most wasted spend hides. Keywords are what you ask for; search terms are what you actually paid for.

Check
A pass
A fail
Search terms
Recent search terms read through, with irrelevant ones added as negatives.
Searches for jobs, free versions, competitors' names or other towns, paid for every month.
Negative keywords
Every Search campaign has a negative list that matches the business, without blocking real customers.
No negatives at all, or so many that the ads barely show. Google warns too many negatives reduce reach.
Broad match with Smart Bidding
Broad match used with a conversion-based Smart Bidding strategy, as Google recommends.
Broad match on Manual CPC, which matches widely with nothing steering it towards conversions.
Quality Score
Keywords that spend have Quality Scores without a Below average component you can fix.
Big spenders scoring 1 to 3, with nobody looking at why.
Worth knowing

Google scores Quality Score from 1 to 10 at the keyword level, built from expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience. It says plainly that Quality Score is not an input in the auction. I use it to point at which part to fix, not as a number to chase.

Enough to test
Each ad group has a responsive search ad using a good share of the 15 headlines and 4 descriptions Google allows.
Ads with a few headlines, all saying the same thing.
Ad Strength
Responsive search ads rated Good or Excellent without losing the message. The full scale is Incomplete, Poor, Average, Good and Excellent.

Targeting and audiences

Settings chosen once at setup and never looked at again cause some of the quietest waste in an account.

Check
A pass
A fail
Location
The location option matches the business. Google calls Presence or interest best practice for Search; a local service that only works in its area often needs Presence instead.
A plumber in Ely paying for clicks from people who searched about Ely from Spain.
Networks
Search campaigns run on search. Display placements, if wanted, have their own campaign and budget.
A Search campaign quietly spending on the Display Network.
Audiences
Search campaigns have relevant audiences added for observation, so you can see how past visitors and customers behave.
No audiences at all, so no insight into returning visitors.
Ad schedule
Call assets are scheduled to show during staffed hours. Google says Smart Bidding respects ad schedules. Call-only ads are being retired, so move any you still use to responsive search ads with call assets before they stop serving in February 2027.
Call assets showing when nobody is available to answer.

Goals
It optimises to the same real conversions as the rest of the account.

Product feed

For shops, the feed is the ad. Shopping and Performance Max build ads from your product data, so the feed decides what shows and for which searches.

Check
A pass
A fail
Titles
Product titles say what the product is, with the words people search for: type, material, colour, size.
Titles that are just a product name nobody searches for.
Product types
Products sorted by type, so performance can be compared by category.
Everything in one group, best sellers and dead stock bidding together.
Custom labels
Custom labels group products by margin, season or best sellers where useful. Standard Shopping also lets you organise bids by product type, brand or item ID.
Products with very different margins grouped together when you need to bid differently for them.
Spend on sellers
Spend goes mainly on products that sell.
A long tail of products taking spend and never selling.

Landing pages

A strong account sending people to a weak page still loses money. The landing page is part of the audit.

Matches the ad

Works on a phone

How to prioritise what you find

A full audit usually turns up more than anyone can fix at once. I sort findings into three groups and work down them in order.

  1. Fix first: anything that corrupts the data. Wrong or duplicated conversions, broken tags, actions used for bidding that don’t record real outcomes. Until these are right, every other change is judged on bad numbers.
  2. Fix next: anything that wastes money now. Irrelevant search terms, campaigns spending with nothing to show, Search campaigns leaking onto Display, location settings that reach the wrong people.
  3. Then: anything that holds back growth. Budget limits on the best campaigns, thin ads and assets, feed titles, landing pages, structure.

Within each group, start with whatever touches the most spend. Then write down each change, the reason and a date to review it. After a major bidding or budget change, allow one to two conversion cycles before judging it, so the next audit can tell what worked.

One rule above the rest

Fix conversion tracking first, always. Smart Bidding, Performance Max and every report learn from it. A beautifully structured account bidding towards the wrong conversions is still spending in the wrong direction.
