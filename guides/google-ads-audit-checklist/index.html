Google Ads Audit Checklist: 9 Areas to Check, in Order

This is the checklist I work through when I take on a Google Ads account. Nine areas, in the order I check them, each with what a pass and a fail look like. Work through it yourself, or use it to check the work of whoever runs your account.

Check my Google Ads

Lewis Phillips · 17 Nov 2026 · Updated 21 Nov 2026 · 11 min read

Quick answer

Audit in this order: conversion tracking, bidding and budget, account structure, keywords and search terms, ads and assets, targeting and audiences, Performance Max, product feed and landing pages. Fix anything that corrupts the data first, then anything that wastes money now, then anything holding back growth. Do a full audit once or twice a year and a short check of tracking, budgets and search terms every month.

Why audit, and how often

Google Ads accounts rarely break in one go. A tracking tag stops firing after a website update, a budget cap that made sense last year stays in place, search terms drift, and nobody notices because the account still spends and still reports something.

An audit is a structured look at the whole account against a fixed list, so problems are found by checking rather than by luck. I do a full one when I take on an account, after big changes such as a new website or new tracking, and once or twice a year after that. In between, a short monthly check of tracking, budgets and search terms catches most problems early.

The nine areas below are in the order I work through them. The order matters: there is no point tuning bids or ads while the account is learning from the wrong conversions.

01 Conversion tracking

Everything else depends on this. Smart Bidding learns from the conversions it sees, so if they are wrong, the bidding chases the wrong thing and every report misleads.

How to prioritise what you find

A full audit usually turns up more than anyone can fix at once. I sort findings into three groups and work down them in order.

  1. Fix first: anything that corrupts the data. Wrong or duplicated conversions, broken tags, actions used for bidding that don’t record real outcomes. Until these are right, every other change is judged on bad numbers.
  2. Fix next: anything that wastes money now. Irrelevant search terms, campaigns spending with nothing to show, Search campaigns leaking onto Display, location settings that reach the wrong people.
  3. Then: anything that holds back growth. Budget limits on the best campaigns, thin ads and assets, feed titles, landing pages, structure.

Within each group, start with whatever touches the most spend. Then write down each change, the reason and a date to review it. After a major bidding or budget change, allow one to two conversion cycles before judging it, so the next audit can tell what worked.

Questions

How often should I audit a Google Ads account?

I do a full audit when I take on an account, after a big change such as a new website or new tracking, and once or twice a year after that. In between, a short monthly check of tracking, budgets and search terms catches most problems early.

What should I check first in a Google Ads audit?

Conversion tracking, always. Smart Bidding and every report depend on it, so if the conversions are wrong, every other finding is built on bad data.

Is Quality Score worth chasing?

Use it as a diagnostic, not a target. Google says Quality Score is not an input in the ad auction. A low score on a keyword that spends points you to the ad, the keyword or the landing page to look at.

Should I use broad match?

Google recommends broad match with Smart Bidding, because it gives the bidding more searches to learn from. I use it where tracking is sound and a conversion-based strategy is running, and I watch the search terms closely.
