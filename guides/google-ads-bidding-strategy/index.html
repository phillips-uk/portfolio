Google Ads Bidding Strategy: Which to Use and When

Lewis Phillips · 14 Nov 2026 · 11 min read

A bidding strategy tells Google what you want your money to buy: conversions, conversion value, clicks or a place on the page. The right one depends on the business goal and on how much you can trust your conversion data, in that order.

Quick answer

If your tracking is reliable and conversions are coming in, I usually start with Maximise conversions, or Maximise conversion value when some conversions are worth more than others. I add a Target CPA or Target ROAS once I know what a customer is worth and the volume is steady. If the tracking isn't reliable, fix it first and use Maximise clicks or Manual CPC in the meantime. Target impression share is for visibility, not conversions.

Pick the goal first, then the strategy

Before changing a bidding setting, decide what a good result looks like. For a local service business that is usually an enquiry or a booking. For an online shop it is sales, and often the value of those sales.

Each strategy works towards a different thing. A clicks strategy has done its job when it brings traffic within the budget, even if nobody enquires. A visibility strategy has done its job when your ad appears where you asked. Only the conversion and value strategies aim at conversions or conversion value.

Those four are the Smart Bidding strategies: Maximise conversions, Target CPA, Maximise conversion value and Target ROAS. They optimise towards your Primary conversion actions, the ones set to “Include in Conversions”. Secondary actions are reported but not bid towards.

That makes your conversion setup part of the bidding decision. If the Primary actions are wrong, duplicated or missing, Smart Bidding optimises towards the wrong thing. I check that those actions are real business outcomes before judging any strategy. If you are not sure yours are right, start with how to fix Google Ads conversion tracking and why GA4 imports can break Smart Bidding.

The options in 2026

Two labels changed in June 2026. “Maximise conversions with a Target CPA” is now “Target CPA”, and “Maximise conversion value with a Target ROAS” is now “Target ROAS”. Google says the bidding behaviour itself is unchanged, and accounts needed no action.

Enhanced CPC has gone from Search and Display. It stopped being available from the week of 24 March 2025, and campaigns that were not moved to another strategy are now effectively on Manual CPC.

Each strategy, and when I use it

01

Maximise conversions

This is where I usually start once tracking is right and the goal is more enquiries, bookings or sales. Google sets bids to get the most conversions while spending your budget, so the budget is the control.

It is designed to spend the full daily budget. If a campaign has been spending well under its budget, switching to Maximise conversions can raise spend sharply, so set the budget at a level you are happy to spend before you switch.

Watch the cost per conversion it produces, and watch what those conversions are. A form submission only matters if it is the kind of enquiry the business wants. I compare the cost per conversion with what that conversion is worth to the business; if the conversion is an enquiry, that means also knowing how often enquiries become customers.

If it costs more to win a customer than the business can support, I look at the offer, the landing page and the targeting as well as the bidding.

02

Target CPA

Target CPA sets bids to get as many conversions as possible at or below the cost you set. I use it when there is a clear, defensible answer to what a lead or sale can cost.

You can start it with no conversion history, using a target based on business goals. Where there is history, Google suggests starting from the last 30 days' average CPA, adjusted for conversion delays.

Start from the evidence. For example, if recent leads have cost about £50 each, a £20 target is unlikely to be a realistic starting point. A target set too tight can hold back volume, so I start close to what the account already achieves and move it in steps.

Google's guidance for judging Target CPA results refers to at least 30 conversions in the last 30 days. That is a reference for evaluation, not a requirement to switch it on.

03

Maximise conversion value and Target ROAS

When conversions are worth different amounts, counting them all the same is a poor fit. An online shop cares about sales value. A lead business might value an enquiry for a full kitchen refit well above one for a small repair.

Maximise conversion value spends the budget to get the most conversion value. Target ROAS predicts the value of potential conversions and sets bids to get as much conversion value as possible at the return on ad spend you set.

The values have to mean something. Use real order values or defensible lead values, and check the assumptions behind them before asking Google to optimise towards them.

For Target ROAS, Google lists at least 15 conversions in the past 30 days for Search and Shopping. Base the target on your business goals and your historical ROAS, which you can work out from the account:

Conv. value ÷ cost × 100 = historical ROAS (%)

For example, £4,000 of conversion value from £1,000 of spend is a 400% ROAS. Whether that is profitable depends on your margins, so check what the values represent before treating it as a good result. Google warns that a target set too high may limit how much traffic your ads get.

04

Maximise clicks and Manual CPC

Maximise clicks sets bids to get as many clicks as possible within the budget. It suits a traffic goal, a new campaign without enough conversion data, or a holding position while tracking is fixed.

Manual CPC lets you set your own maximum cost per click for each ad group, with keyword-level bids if you want them. It suits someone who wants that control and has the time to use it.

Neither aims at conversions. If the real goal is enquiries or sales, give either one a clear purpose and a date to review it.

Enhanced CPC is no longer an option for Search and Display. If an older guide recommends it, that advice is out of date.

05

Target impression share

Target impression share is for a deliberate visibility goal, such as aiming to appear prominently when someone searches your business name.

You choose where to aim: absolute top (the first ad above the organic results), top (above the organic results) or anywhere on the results page. It works on the Search Network only, and its impression share excludes Search Partners.

It has a maximum CPC bid limit, and Google warns against setting it too low. Choose a limit that makes sense for the visibility you are buying, then judge the campaign on that. It aims for the position, not for conversions.

When conversions are thin

If the business only gets a few conversions a month, plan around that, rather than expecting the account to behave like a large online retailer's.

First, check the Primary actions. For an enquiry-led business that usually means calls, form submissions and bookings. Don't make page views or other light actions Primary just to raise the count: if page views are Primary, Smart Bidding optimises towards page views.

Next, look at the structure. Splitting a small budget across many campaigns spreads the same few conversions more thinly, so I keep campaigns few and focused unless there is a clear commercial reason to split them.

Then start with Maximise conversions before adding a target, and judge results over longer windows. With a handful of enquiries a month, one extra conversion can make a short period look very different.

Keep the conversion meaningful

A small number of real enquiries is a better signal than a large number of actions nobody would pay for. Check what the conversion count represents before trying to increase it.

Changing strategy without starting from scratch

Not every change sends a strategy back to learning. Changes to budgets, targets, creative or targeting settings don't remove historical data and don't trigger Google's “Learning” status.

That status can appear after a new bid strategy, a change in what the strategy covers (campaigns, ad groups or keywords added or removed), or a significant change to conversion goals or actions. Applying a new portfolio strategy can show it for up to seven days.

The status never lasts more than seven days, but performance takes one to two conversion cycles to settle. A conversion cycle is roughly how long it usually takes for a click to become a conversion. I judge results over two conversion cycles or two weeks, whichever is longer.

Move targets in steps rather than big jumps, so you can tell whether each new target is realistic. And don't judge a change on the next morning's numbers.

Keep cash flow in view: on a given day, spend can reach up to twice the average daily budget. Google also advises against bid limits on Target CPA and Target ROAS, so I don't add them as a routine precaution.

Two tools for unusual weeks

Seasonality adjustments tell Smart Bidding to expect a major change in conversion rate during a short future event, such as a sale. Google says normal seasonality is already handled, so keep them for big, expected changes.

They work best for events of one to seven days and may work less well beyond 14 days. They are available for Search, Shopping, Demand Gen and Display campaigns using Target CPA or Target ROAS, and for Performance Max with all strategies.

Data exclusions do a different job. When tracking breaks (a broken tag, a website outage or a failed import), they reduce the impact on Smart Bidding for the period you select.

They apply to clicks in that period and don't change your conversion reporting. They work with conversion and value-based Smart Bidding in Search, Display, Shopping and Performance Max. Google advises against using them often or for long periods.

A simple way to choose

I work through these in order:

  1. Is the tracking right? If not, fix it. Use Maximise clicks or Manual CPC if campaigns need to keep running meanwhile.
  2. Are useful conversions coming in? If so, Maximise conversions is my usual starting point.
  3. Are some conversions worth more than others? If the values are real, consider Maximise conversion value.
  4. Do you know your target? With a realistic cost per acquisition or return target and steady volume, consider Target CPA or Target ROAS.
  5. Is the goal visibility? If visibility on Search is what you need, consider Target impression share.

Then judge the result against the goal you started with. Keep a strategy because it brings useful results at a cost the business can support, not because its name sounds more advanced.
