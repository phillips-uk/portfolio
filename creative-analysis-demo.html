#1 summer_prospecting_ugc-hero.mp4 [opening]
7.2 / 10
DimensionScoreRationale
Hook quality 8 Close-up motion in the opening frame creates an immediate pattern interrupt. The on-screen text caption loads within the first second, giving the viewer a clear reason to stay.
Visual hierarchy 7 Subject occupies the upper two-thirds of the frame with negative space used cleanly below. Reading order is intuitive.
Weak signal: Background element in bottom-right competes with the subject for attention.
Copy clarity 7 The headline caption is legible and specific. The value proposition is clear in under two seconds. No explicit CTA text is present in this frame, which is acceptable for an opening hook.
Brand consistency 6 Amber overlay text matches product palette but no logo or consistent brand lockup visible in this frame.
Weak signal: No logo or brand lockup. This creative could belong to any brand.
Fatigue signals 8 Format avoids common fatigued treatments. No plain white background, no static talking head with no hook variant. The motion-first approach is less saturated than most UGC in this category.
Overall notes The opening frame lands a strong pattern interrupt with close-up motion and an on-screen text hook. Visual hierarchy is clean. The main gap is brand anchoring. Priority fix: add a subtle branded element to the bottom-left corner without obscuring the hook.
#2 summer_prospecting_static-offer.jpg
6.6 / 10
DimensionScoreRationale
Hook quality 6 Product is clearly visible and the offer is readable, but there is no pattern interrupt to stop a scrolling thumb. Static format with no motion, contrast surprise, or emotional trigger.
Weak signal: Static product shot with no scroll-stopping element. Likely to be skipped on mobile feed.
Visual hierarchy 7 Product is the clear focal point. Headline and price point sit naturally below it. Clean layout with adequate white space. No competing visual elements.
Copy clarity 8 Offer is specific and immediately legible. Discount percentage and end date are both visible. No ambiguity about what is being sold or what the deal is.
Brand consistency 7 Brand colours applied consistently. Logo positioned correctly. Font choice matches the brand system. Minor inconsistency in the CTA button colour weight.
Fatigue signals 5 Plain white background product shot is one of the most overused formats in paid social. The format has been saturated for two-plus years across e-commerce categories.
Weak signal: Plain white background product format. High fatigue risk — audiences have learned to skip this format.
Overall notes Strong on clarity and brand, weak on attention capture. The copy does its job but the format will not stop the scroll. The fatigue signal is the main risk here. Test a lifestyle or contextual background version of this offer against the plain white to measure the CTR delta.
#3 retargeting_abandoned-cart_v2.jpg
5.6 / 10
DimensionScoreRationale
Hook quality 5 Retargeting context means the audience already knows the product, which reduces the hook burden slightly. Even so, the creative does not leverage that recognition with a specific reengagement angle.
Weak signal: No urgency or reengagement hook. Retargeting creatives need a specific reason to return, not just a product reminder.
Visual hierarchy 6 Product is identifiable but the composition has two competing focal points: the product image and a text overlay of equal visual weight.
Weak signal: Two competing focal points at equal weight. The eye does not know where to go first.
Copy clarity 7 The reminder message is clear. "You left something behind" framing is specific and relevant to an abandoned cart audience. Message lands in under two seconds.
Brand consistency 6 Brand colours are present but the text weight used on the overlay is lighter than the brand system standard, creating a visual inconsistency.
Weak signal: Text weight inconsistency with brand system. Feels off-brand at a glance.
Fatigue signals 4 Generic "you left something" format with product image and text overlay is among the most replicated retargeting formats. No differentiating visual treatment.
Weak signal: Overused retargeting format with no visual differentiation. Audiences in this segment will have seen near-identical creatives from competitors.
Overall notes The copy angle is right for a retargeting audience but the execution is generic. Fatigue risk is high. The format needs a visual differentiator: a time-limited offer, social proof element, or a format change (video or UGC) to separate it from the noise in this audience segment.
#4 retargeting_generic-cta.jpg
3.8 / 10
DimensionScoreRationale
Hook quality 3 No visual trigger, no text hook, no motion. The creative opens on a product shot with a logo and a CTA button. There is nothing to stop a scroll.
Weak signal: No hook element of any kind. The creative assumes the audience will stop voluntarily.
Visual hierarchy 4 Logo, product, headline, and CTA button all compete at similar visual weights. No single element dominates. Reading order is unclear.
Weak signal: Four elements at equal weight with no clear visual priority. The reader's eye has nowhere to land.
Copy clarity 5 The product name is legible but the headline does not state a benefit or offer. The CTA is "Shop Now" with no supporting context.
Weak signal: "Shop Now" CTA with no offer, benefit, or urgency. Gives the audience no reason to act now rather than later.
Brand consistency 4 Brand colours are present but the layout does not follow the brand's established visual system. Feels like a template rather than a brand asset.
Weak signal: Template-style layout with no branded personality. Indistinguishable from competitor creative at a glance.
Fatigue signals 3 Logo-first layout, plain background, generic "Shop Now" CTA. This creative hits three of the five most common ad fatigue indicators simultaneously.
Weak signal: Logo-first layout + plain background + generic CTA. Three high-fatigue signals in one creative.
Overall notes This creative has no hook, no visual hierarchy, and no specific offer. It is built around brand visibility rather than performance. For paid social, where the audience did not choose to see the ad, every element needs to earn its place. This creative needs a complete rebuild starting from the hook. The brand assets are present — the problem is how they are deployed.
