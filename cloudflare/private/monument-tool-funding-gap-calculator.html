    Questions about this calculator

    How do you calculate a working capital funding gap?

    Estimate the trade debtors and stock needed at your target turnover and subtract expected trade creditors. Do the same at current turnover, then take the current requirement away from the target requirement. The difference is the extra working capital the growth would require.

    What is overtrading?

    Overtrading is when a company grows beyond the working capital it has available. It can happen when more sales mean buying stock or paying suppliers before the new invoices are collected. A profitable order book can still leave the company short of cash.

    Can asset-based lending cover a funding gap?

    Asset-based lending can cover part of a funding gap where the company has eligible debtors or stock. The amount depends on those assets and the lender’s advance rates. Compare the estimate with the full gap to see how much may need to come from other sources.

    How to estimate the cost of growth

    Calculate working capital at both levels of sales

    Start with current annual turnover, target annual turnover and gross margin. At each level, find cost of sales by multiplying turnover by one minus the gross margin. Estimate trade debtors by multiplying turnover by debtor days and dividing by 365. Estimate stock by multiplying cost of sales by stock days and dividing by 365. Use the same method with creditor days to estimate trade creditors.

    Add debtors and stock, then subtract trade creditors. That gives the estimated working capital requirement at each level. Subtract the current requirement from the target requirement to get the funding gap. The calculation holds gross margin and the three day counts steady.

    Illustration using round numbers

    A company plans to grow annual turnover from £3.65m to £7.3m at a gross margin of 50%. It collects invoices in 40 days, holds stock for 30 days and pays suppliers in 20 days. At current turnover, it needs £400,000 for trade debtors and £150,000 for stock, offset by £100,000 of trade creditors. That leaves £450,000 tied up in working capital.

    At target turnover, with the same margin and days, estimated trade debtors are £800,000 and stock is £300,000. Trade creditors offset £200,000, leaving £900,000 tied up. The extra working capital required is £450,000.

    Check when the extra cash is needed

    The estimate compares two levels of trading. Use a cash flow forecast to see when the extra cash is needed, particularly if stock must be bought before the first sales or if new customers get longer terms. Check the impact of slower collections or more stock by changing the day counts and running the estimate again.

    A facility against eligible trade debtors and stock may fund part of the growth. Look at when those assets will be available to support borrowing and how much cash you need before then. This gives you a clearer figure to discuss with Ben.
