        Debtor days are trade debtors divided by credit sales, times 365.

        The cost is what it takes to fund your trade debtors for a year, at an example finance rate of 8%.

        Industry and region figures are medians of large UK companies' own payment practices reports for 2025, published on GOV.UK under the Open Government Licence v3.0, with each company's industry from Companies House. The broad industry figures match the Department for Business and Trade's published ones to within a day.

        A region is where companies have their registered office, from ONS postcode data. That is not always where they trade: many groups register in London.

        A named customer's figures are its own latest report. On the live site all of these refresh every month.

        Invoice discounting turns unpaid invoices into cash as you raise them. It is often part of an asset-based lending facility. Example advance rate for this draft: 70% to 85% of debtors.

            How it works

            The cycle is debtor days plus stock days, less creditor days. Debtor days use turnover; stock and creditor days use cost of sales.

              When growth needs more cash than the extra sales bring in, a business can run out of money while it is winning work. That is overtrading. A facility tied to debtors and stock grows with the sales ledger.

              Questions finance directors ask

              Where do the figures come from?

              GOV.UK payment practices reports and Companies House filed accounts and charges. They reflect the periods those records cover.

              Is my company’s data shared?

              The figures you type stay in your browser and reach Ben only if you request terms. Company searches use public records and are not linked to you.

              How accurate are the results?

              They are estimates from the figures and assumptions in each tool. Lenders set their own terms.
